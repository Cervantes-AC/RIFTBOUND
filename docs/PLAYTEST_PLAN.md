# Playtest plan (Week 5, lab Phase 8)

Goal: at least **5 players outside the team** play the game, the team collects structured feedback, fixes the top
problems (**at least 3 real improvements**) and **retests with at least 2 new players**.

**Status: NOT YET RUN.** No participants have played the game yet; `PLAYTEST_RESULTS.md` is an empty template.
Nothing in these documents may be filled in until real testers have completed the forms.

## Preparation

* Phone with the latest APK, **fresh install** (Settings -> *Reset progress*, or reinstall) so Level 1 is the only unlocked level.
* A second phone for the online match, both on the same Wi-Fi (or one phone's hotspot).
* Printed or online copy of the observation sheet and feedback form (Google Form or a spreadsheet).
* Remove testers' names from anything saved to the repository (anonymise: Tester 1, Tester 2 ...).

## Session (15-20 minutes per player)

1. One-sentence briefing only: *"Please play as you normally would and say out loud what you are thinking."* Do not explain controls or goals - the game's tutorial hints and objective text must do that.
2. Player plays **Levels 1-3 offline**, then **one online match** against a team member.
3. Observer fills the observation sheet silently. Help only if the player is stuck for more than 2 minutes; note that help was given.
4. Player fills the feedback form immediately afterwards.

## Observation sheet (per tester, per level)

| Tester no. | Level | Attempts to clear | Time to clear | Where stuck/confused | Quotes | Bugs seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rows for Level 1, 2, 3 and Online for each tester.

## Feedback form

Tester information: age range (under 18 / 18-24 / 25+), mobile-game frequency (daily / weekly / rarely).

Part A - rate 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree):

1. The game was fun to play.
2. I understood the goal of each level without help.
3. The touch controls were easy and responsive.
4. Each level felt a little harder than the last, but fair.
5. Unlocking the next level made me want to keep playing.
6. The win/lose result screen clearly showed how I did.
7. Offline mode worked smoothly.
8. Joining an online match was easy.
9. The online match felt responsive (little or no lag).
10. The graphics were clear and attractive.
11. The sound and music fit the game.
12. Text and buttons were easy to read and tap.
13. The game ran without crashes or freezes.
14. I would play this game again.
15. I would recommend this game to a friend.

Part B - open questions: what did you like most; most frustrating/confusing moment; which level was too easy/hard and why;
anything that did not work as expected; the one thing you would change; overall rating (1-5 stars).

## Analysis rules

* Average each Part A statement; **below 3.5 = problem area**.
* Per level: completion rate (cleared / tried) and average attempts. Fewer than 60 % completion or more than 4 attempts on average = probably too hard (except the final level).
* Group open answers into themes (controls, difficulty, clarity, bugs, online, art/sound) and count mentions.
* Fix by severity (Critical = crash/lost progress/cannot finish; Major = confusing goal, unfair difficulty, online fails often; Minor = cosmetic) then mentions.
* Retest the fixes with at least 2 new testers and record the result in the action-plan table.

## Things worth watching (hypotheses from development, NOT findings)

* Is the Level 1 tutorial text enough without verbal help?
* Are the six right-hand touch buttons comfortable on small phones?
* Is Level 3 (ice + kiting ranger) and Level 5 (boss) too hard for casual players?
* Does the online match feel responsive on the school Wi-Fi?
